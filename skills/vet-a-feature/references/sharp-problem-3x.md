# Sharpness — the 3-question test + the ≥3x bar (Ch 1, pp.14–23)

Run on the **feature's problem**, in order. A SHARP problem passes all three.

## 1. Alternatives

What does the customer do today — even duct-tape or manual? Push past the first answer;
people undercount alternatives. **If a trivial workaround exists (copy/paste, a
spreadsheet that already works), the problem is NOT sharp.** (Calendly refused
"email your link from the app" for exactly this reason — Ch 1, p18.)

## 2. Prevalence — four sub-vectors

- **Market size** — how many people/companies hit it.
- **Growth** — getting worse (good), flat, or shrinking (kill it).
- **Niche vs broad** — both can win; niche = smaller market, often higher value/user.
- **Frequency** — rare → multiple-per-day. Frequency matters more than people think: a
  daily problem sustains a business at a fraction of the market a monthly one needs.

## 3. Value

Are people (or their managers) willing to pay? Estimate two ways: (a) current spend on
alternatives, (b) time saved × hourly cost. Name the **buyer** — not always the user.

## The ≥3x improvement bar

The proposed feature must offer **≥3x** improvement over the status-quo workflow to
overcome switching costs.

- **B2B software:** ≥3x is the floor.
- **Saturated markets** (super-computing, solar): much smaller gains suffice — note the
  market norm before applying the bar.
- **Consumer / new-category:** 10x is often the real bar.

## Verdict

- **SHARP** — all three pass AND ≥3x met.
- **NOT_SHARP** — any axis fails, or a trivial alternative exists.
- **NEEDS_MORE_EVIDENCE** — plausible but a critical input is missing (no target, no
  named alternatives, no frequency). Don't default here — prefer a real call.
